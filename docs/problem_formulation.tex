\section{Introduction and Problem Overview}
%=============================================================================

We formalize the \textbf{Electric Truck Fleet Routing and Charging with Shared Charging Resources} (ETFRC-SCR) problem. This is \emph{not} a classical Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP): delivery sequences are pre-assigned to each truck, and the decisions concern \emph{when}, \emph{where}, and \emph{how long} to charge. The combinatorial and stochastic complexity arises from:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item limited battery range requiring intermediate charging stops,
    \item shared charging infrastructure with finite ports and FCFS queuing,
    \item exogenous uncertainty in travel times, energy consumption, and unloading durations,
    \item endogenous uncertainty from fleet-level charging contention.
\end{enumerate}
The objective is to \textbf{minimize total elapsed time} (travel $+$ charging $+$ waiting $+$ unloading) while completing all deliveries for all trucks, subject to energy feasibility and charging infrastructure constraints.

%=============================================================================
\section{Problem Definition}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Road Network and Infrastructure}

\paragraph{Graph.}
We operate on a directed road network $G=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})$ constructed from real geographic data. Nodes partition into charging stations $\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{V}$ and delivery locations $\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{V}$ (with possible overlap). For each arc $(u,v)\in\mathcal{E}$, pre-computed shortest-path dictionaries provide the deterministic base energy $e_{uv}$ (kWh) and base travel time $\tau_{uv}$ (hours). A base speed $v_0=40$ km/h is used for time computation.

\paragraph{Charging stations.}
Each station $c\in\mathcal{C}$ is characterized by:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \emph{Charger type} $\theta_c\in\{\text{Level\,2},\,\text{DC\,Fast}\}$,
    \item \emph{Peak power} $r_c$ (kW) and \emph{efficiency} $\eta_c$,
    \item \emph{Port capacity} $\kappa_c\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$ --- the number of trucks that can charge simultaneously.
\end{itemize}
Level\,2 chargers operate at 50 kW with 85\% efficiency; DC Fast chargers operate at 50 kW peak with 85\% efficiency but support SOC-dependent power tapering (CCCV model; see~\S\ref{sec:charging-curve}).

\paragraph{Fleet.}
We control a fleet $\mathcal{T}=\{1,\dots,T\}$ of homogeneous electric trucks, each with battery capacity $B=400$ kWh and a minimum state-of-charge buffer $\underline{b}=0.1B=40$ kWh. Initial battery $b_i^0$ is either ``full'' ($=B$) or sampled uniformly from $[0.3B,B]$.

\paragraph{Deliveries.}
Each truck $i$ starts at a depot node $d_{i0}$ and must serve an ordered sequence of deliveries $\mathcal{D}_i = (d_{i1},\dots,d_{iK_i})$, where $K_i$ is the number of stops. In \emph{sequential mode}, deliveries must be visited in order; in \emph{flexible mode}, the truck may visit any remaining delivery. Delivery sequences are generated at episode start by sampling pairs of nodes with hop energy in $[e_{\min},e_{\max}]$ (defaults: $100$--$250$ kWh), and are validated to ensure every leg is completable (possibly via intermediate charger stops). When variable-stop mode is active, $K_i$ is sampled uniformly from $\{1,\dots,K_{\max}\}$ per episode.

\subsection{Objective}

Minimize the total wall-clock time for all trucks to complete their delivery sequences:
\[
\min \; \sum_{i \in \mathcal{T}} \Big( \underbrace{\sum_{k} \tilde{\tau}_{k}^{(i)}}_{\text{travel}} + \underbrace{\sum_{k} h_{k}^{(i)}}_{\text{charging}} + \underbrace{\sum_{k} w_{k}^{(i)}}_{\text{waiting}} + \underbrace{\sum_{k} \tilde{u}_{k}^{(i)}}_{\text{unloading}} \Big),
\]
where tilde ($\tilde{\cdot}$) denotes stochastic quantities. In the RL formulation, this is encoded as a cumulative reward with per-hour time penalties.

\subsection{Decision Variables (Actions)}\label{sec:actions}

At each decision epoch, the active truck $i$ selects from a state-dependent feasible set $\mathcal{A}(s)$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lp{10cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Action type} & \textbf{Description} \\
\midrule
Navigate to charger $c\in\mathcal{C}$ & Route to charging station $c$ \\
Navigate to delivery $d\in R_i$ & Go to next delivery (sequential) or any remaining delivery (flexible) \\
Charge for $h\in\mathcal{H}$ hours & Charge at current location; $\mathcal{H}=\{1,2,\dots,12\}$; only valid at $n_i\in\mathcal{C}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Constraints}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Battery bounds}: $\underline{b} \le b_i \le B$ at all times (buffer $\underline{b}=0.1B$).
    \item \textbf{Energy feasibility}: Navigation to node $v$ requires $\alpha\, e_{n_i,v} < b_i$, where $\alpha$ is a worst-case energy safety multiplier (default $\alpha = \bar{\xi} = 1.20$).
    \item \textbf{Post-arrival feasibility}: A delivery action is allowed only if, after arriving at the delivery node with worst-case energy depletion, the truck can still reach at least one charger.
    \item \textbf{Charger capacity gating}: At most $\kappa_c$ trucks may charge simultaneously at station $c$; additional trucks enter an FCFS waitlist (see~\S\ref{sec:queuing}).
    \item \textbf{Charger vacation}: After completing a charge session, a truck \emph{must} leave the port (cannot immediately re-charge) to allow waiting trucks to access the port.
    \item \textbf{Time horizon}: Episode truncates at $T_{\max}=200$ hours or at a step cap (safety bound).
\end{enumerate}

%=============================================================================
\section{Sources of Uncertainty}\label{sec:uncertainty}
%=============================================================================

The environment features both \emph{exogenous} and \emph{endogenous} stochasticity. All exogenous random variables use seeded, cached RNGs with deterministic hash-based keys for reproducibility across algorithm comparisons.

\subsection{Travel Time (Traffic)}

Travel time on arc $(u,v)$ departing at global time $g$ is:
\[
\tilde{\tau}_{uv} \sim \mathcal{N}\!\Big(\tau_{uv},\;\sigma_{uv}^2(g)\Big), \qquad \tilde{\tau}_{uv} \in \big[0.85\,\tau_{uv},\; 2.5\,\tau_{uv}\big].
\]
The time-of-day dependent standard deviation is:
\[
\sigma_{uv}(g) = \min\!\Big(\sigma_{\max},\; \sigma_0\,\tau_{uv}\,\big(1 + (\rho-1)\cdot f_{\text{rush}}(g, g+\tau_{uv})\big)\Big),
\]
where $\sigma_0=0.15$ is the base fractional std-dev, $\rho=2.0$ is the rush-hour variance multiplier, $\sigma_{\max}=1.0$ hour is the std-dev cap, and $f_{\text{rush}}(g_1,g_2)\in[0,1]$ is the fraction of the \emph{journey interval} $[g_1,g_2]$ that overlaps rush hours (07:00--09:00, 17:00--19:00). Importantly, $f_{\text{rush}}$ is computed over the journey duration, not just at departure.

\subsection{Energy Consumption}

Energy consumption is \emph{correlated with traffic conditions}:
\[
\tilde{e}_{uv} = e_{uv}\;\xi_{uv}, \qquad \xi_{uv}\in[\underline{\xi},\overline{\xi}] = [0.90,\,1.20],
\]
with:
\[
\xi_{uv} = \operatorname{clip}\!\Big(\underbrace{1 + 0.5\,(\tilde{\tau}_{uv}/\tau_{uv} - 1)}_{\text{traffic-correlated bias}} + \varepsilon ,\; \underline{\xi},\;\overline{\xi}\Big), \qquad \varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\,\sigma_e^2), \quad \sigma_e = 0.05\,e_{uv}.
\]
Worse traffic (higher $\tilde{\tau}_{uv}/\tau_{uv}$) systematically increases energy consumption, reflecting stop-and-go inefficiency. The safety multiplier $\alpha=\overline{\xi}=1.20$ used in feasibility masking accounts for this worst case.

\subsection{Unloading Time}

Service time at delivery $d$ at global time $g$ is:
\[
\tilde{u}_d \sim \mathcal{N}\!\Big(u_0,\;\sigma_u^2(g)\Big), \qquad \tilde{u}_d \in \big[0.75\,u_0,\; 1.50\,u_0\big],
\]
with base duration $u_0=0.2$ hours (12 minutes). Business hours (09:00--17:00) increase variance:
\[
\sigma_u(g) = \min\!\big(\sigma_u^{\max},\; s_u\,u_0\,\mu_{\text{biz}}(g)\big), \qquad \mu_{\text{biz}}(g) = \begin{cases} 1.5 & \text{if } 9\le(g\!\!\mod 24)<17,\\ 1.0 & \text{otherwise},\end{cases}
\]
where $s_u=0.20$ and $\sigma_u^{\max}=0.25$ hours. This captures variability from dock availability, facility congestion, and staffing.

\subsection{Endogenous Charging Contention}\label{sec:queuing}

While not a random variable, charger queuing introduces \emph{endogenous stochasticity}: waiting time at a station depends on the arrival and charging decisions of \emph{other} trucks in the fleet. A truck arriving at a station with all $\kappa_c$ ports occupied enters an FCFS waitlist and is event-driven ``woken'' when a port frees. This creates hard-to-predict delays that depend on the joint policy.

%=============================================================================
\section{Modeling Realisms}\label{sec:realisms}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{Realistic Charging Curves (CCCV)}\label{sec:charging-curve}

DC Fast chargers implement a four-phase \textbf{Constant Current -- Constant Voltage (CCCV)} model calibrated to the 2024 Chevrolet Silverado EV charging profile. Given an initial SOC $s_0$, charge duration $h$, peak power $P_{\text{peak}}$, and efficiency $\eta$, the instantaneous power $P(s)$ depends on the current SOC $s$:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcl@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Phase} & \textbf{SOC range} & \textbf{Power} \\
\midrule
Ramp-up & $0\text{--}10\%$ & $P = P_{\text{peak}}\,(0.6 + 3.0\,s)$ \quad (60\% $\to$ 90\% of peak) \\
Ramp continued & $10\text{--}50\%$ & $P = P_{\text{peak}}\,(0.9 + 0.25\,(s - 0.1))$ \quad (90\% $\to$ 100\%) \\
Plateau (CC) & $50\text{--}80\%$ & $P = P_{\text{peak}}$ \\
Taper (CV) & $80\text{--}100\%$ & $P = P_{\text{peak}}\,\big(1 - 0.6\,p^{1.5}\big)$, \; $p = \frac{s - 0.8}{0.2}$, \; $P \ge P_{\min}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The charge is computed via numerical integration with time step $\Delta t_{\text{sim}} = 0.01$ h (36 seconds):
\[
\Delta E = P(s)\,\eta\,\Delta t_{\text{sim}}, \qquad s \leftarrow s + \frac{\Delta E}{B}.
\]
The average taper factor (ratio of average to peak power) is approximately $89.4\%$ over a full 0--100\% session. Level\,2 chargers use a simple linear model: $\Delta E = P_{\text{peak}}\,\eta\,h$, clamped at $100\%$ SOC.

\subsection{Charger Queuing with FCFS Gating}

Each charging station $c$ maintains:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item An \emph{occupancy list} of truck IDs currently charging (size $\le \kappa_c$),
    \item A \emph{waitlist} (FCFS queue with global sequence numbers) of trucks awaiting a port.
\end{itemize}
When a port frees (truck finishes charging), the station's \texttt{wake\_waiting\_trucks} method issues an immediate \textsc{TRUCK\_READY} event for the first truck in the waitlist, enabling it to begin charging. Trucks currently routing are skipped. This is purely event-driven --- no polling or time-stepping is involved.

\subsection{Time-of-Day Traffic}

The traffic model accounts for time-of-day effects by computing the \emph{rush hour fraction} $f_{\text{rush}}(g_1,g_2)$ over the entire journey interval $[g_1,g_2]$, not just at departure. This is evaluated by sampling the interval at 6-minute resolution and checking overlap with rush-hour windows (07:00--09:00 and 17:00--19:00).

\subsection{Delivery Sequence Feasibility Validation}

At truck creation, delivery sequences are validated to ensure every leg is completable (possibly via intermediate charger stops) under worst-case energy consumption. If a leg is infeasible, the hop distance bounds are progressively reduced until a feasible sequence is found.

%=============================================================================
\section{Event-Driven Simulation}\label{sec:simulation}
%=============================================================================

The environment uses an \textbf{event-driven simulation} with a global clock $g$ and a priority queue of events $\mathcal{Q}$. This avoids fixed-timestep discretization and naturally handles heterogeneous action durations.

\paragraph{Event types.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textsc{TRUCK\_READY}: Decision epoch --- the truck requires an action from the controller.
    \item \textsc{TRUCK\_ROUTING}: Arrival event --- the truck has completed travel and reached its destination.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Truck state machine.}
Each truck $i$ inhabits one of six states:
\[
\sigma_i \in \{\texttt{ready},\; \texttt{routing},\; \texttt{waiting\_to\_charge},\; \texttt{charging},\; \texttt{unloading},\; \texttt{complete},\; \texttt{failed}\}.
\]
Transitions follow the state machine:
\begin{center}
\texttt{ready} $\xrightarrow{\text{navigate}}$ \texttt{routing} $\xrightarrow{\text{arrive}}$ \texttt{ready} or \texttt{waiting\_to\_charge}

\texttt{waiting\_to\_charge} $\xrightarrow{\text{port available}}$ \texttt{ready} $\xrightarrow{\text{charge}}$ \texttt{charging} $\xrightarrow{\text{done}}$ \texttt{ready}

\texttt{ready} $\xrightarrow{\text{deliver}}$ \texttt{unloading} $\xrightarrow{\text{done}}$ \texttt{ready}

\texttt{ready} $\xrightarrow{\text{all done}}$ \texttt{complete} \quad or \quad \texttt{routing} $\xrightarrow{b_i < 0}$ \texttt{failed}
\end{center}

\paragraph{Episode flow.}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item All trucks initialized at random start nodes with delivery sequences and initial batteries.
    \item The global clock advances to the next event in $\mathcal{Q}$ (minimum-time extraction).
    \item Only the \textbf{active truck} (whose event fires) requires a decision --- yielding a \emph{single-agent control paradigm} over a multi-truck fleet.
    \item The chosen action triggers stochastic state transitions (travel/$\tilde{\tau}$, energy/$\tilde{e}$, charging/CCCV curve, unloading/$\tilde{u}$, or queuing).
    \item New events are pushed onto $\mathcal{Q}$; loop repeats until all trucks are \texttt{complete}/\texttt{failed}, or $g > T_{\max}$.
\end{enumerate}

%=============================================================================
\section{Markov Decision Process Formulation}\label{sec:mdp}
%=============================================================================

The single-agent controller observes a partially aggregated multi-truck system where only the active truck can act at each decision epoch.

\subsection{State Space $\mathcal{S}$}

\[
s = \big(g,\; i_{\text{act}},\; \{(n_j, b_j, \sigma_j, R_j)\}_{j\in\mathcal{T}},\; \chi\big),
\]
where $g$ is the global clock, $i_{\text{act}}$ the active truck index, $n_j$ the current node of truck $j$, $b_j$ its battery level, $\sigma_j$ its state, $R_j$ its remaining deliveries, and $\chi$ encodes charger occupancy, waitlists, and pending events.

\paragraph{Heterogeneous graph observation.}
The observation is encoded as a PyTorch Geometric \texttt{HeteroData} graph with three node types:

\medskip
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcp{8.5cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Node type} & \textbf{Dim} & \textbf{Features} \\
\midrule
Truck & 14 & node type, position, battery (kWh \& \%), state one-hot (ready/routing/waiting/charging), deliveries completed \& remaining, elapsed time (normalized), distance traveled, time to destination, must\_leave\_charger flag \\
Delivery & 3 & node type, node ID (normalized), sequence index (normalized) \\
Charger & 4 & node type, node ID (normalized), occupancy rate (current/capacity), queue length (normalized) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
\medskip

Nine edge types connect the node types: truck$\leftrightarrow$delivery, truck$\leftrightarrow$charger, charger$\leftrightarrow$charger, charger$\leftrightarrow$delivery, delivery$\leftrightarrow$delivery, and truck$\leftrightarrow$truck. Each edge carries features $[\,e/1000,\;\tau/T_{\max}\,]$ (normalized energy and time). Edges are added only if the traversal is energy-feasible (accounting for the safety factor $\alpha$). Completed/failed trucks and delivered nodes are excluded from the graph.

Two global features are appended: normalized global time $g / T_{\max}$ and the active truck ID.

\subsection{Action Space $\mathcal{A}(s)$}

Discrete, state-dependent with feasibility masking. Actions are indexed as:
\[
\underbrace{a_0,\dots,a_{|\mathcal{C}|-1}}_{\text{navigate to chargers}} ,\;\; \underbrace{a_{|\mathcal{C}|},\dots}_{\text{navigate to deliveries}} ,\;\; \underbrace{a_{\cdot},\dots,a_{\cdot+|\mathcal{H}|-1}}_{\text{charge durations}},
\]
where in sequential mode there is one delivery action (next in sequence) and in flexible mode one per remaining delivery. A return-to-depot action may also be present.

\paragraph{Feasibility masking (key contribution).}
The mask $m(s)\in\{0,1\}^{|\mathcal{A}|}$ enforces:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Energy headroom}: $\alpha\,e_{n_i,v} < b_i$ for navigation to $v$.
    \item \textbf{Post-arrival feasibility}: For deliveries, $\exists\, c\in\mathcal{C}$ such that the truck can reach $c$ from $v$ with the remaining battery after travel.
    \item \textbf{Location constraints}: Charging only at $n_i\in\mathcal{C}$; self-loops disallowed.
    \item \textbf{Charger vacation}: After charging, charging actions are blocked (must vacate port).
    \item \textbf{Terminal exclusion}: No actions for \texttt{complete}/\texttt{failed} trucks.
\end{enumerate}
The mask accounts for worst-case energy via $\alpha$ and prevents the policy from ever selecting an action that would strand the truck.

\subsection{Transition Kernel $\mathcal{P}$}

Transitions are event-driven and stochastic:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Navigation}: Travel completes after $\tilde{\tau}_{uv}$ hours with battery update $b'\!=\!b - \tilde{e}_{uv}$. If $b'<0$, the truck enters \texttt{failed} state.
    \item \textbf{Charging}: SOC updates via CCCV or linear model for $h$ hours. The actual energy added and final SOC are returned by the charging curve model.
    \item \textbf{Charger gating}: If no port is free, the truck enters \texttt{waiting\_to\_charge}. A \textsc{TRUCK\_READY} event is scheduled when a port frees (FCFS).
    \item \textbf{Unloading}: Holds the truck for $\tilde{u}_d$ hours, then transitions to \texttt{ready}.
    \item The next decision epoch occurs at the earliest \textsc{TRUCK\_READY} event in $\mathcal{Q}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Reward}

\[
r(s,a,s') = \underbrace{-\lambda_{\text{time}}\,\Delta t}_{\text{time penalty}} + \underbrace{R_{\text{del}}\cdot\mathbf{1}[\text{delivery completed}]}_{\text{delivery bonus}} + \underbrace{R_{\text{fail}}\cdot\mathbf{1}[\text{failure}]}_{\text{failure penalty}} + \underbrace{(-\Delta t_{\text{wait}})}_{\text{waiting penalty}},
\]
with defaults $\lambda_{\text{time}}=1.0$, $R_{\text{del}}=+500$, $R_{\text{fail}}=-1000$. The waiting penalty captures queuing delays buffered from charger contention and is applied to the truck's next action. An optional leftover battery penalty $-\lambda_{\text{SOC}}\cdot(\text{remaining SOC}\%)$ is available but disabled by default.

\section{Deterministic Per-Truck Baseline (Gurobi MILP)}\label{sec:gurobi}
%=============================================================================

The baseline planner optimizes one truck at a time, ignoring charger contention and using nominal (deterministic) travel times. Let the remaining delivery sequence for truck $i$ be $(d_0,\dots,d_K)$ with $d_0$ the current node. For each leg $k=0,\dots,K-1$, options are to go directly or detour via one charger $c\in\mathcal{C}$.

\subsection*{Parameters}
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $B$: battery capacity; $b^{\text{init}}$: current energy.
    \item $\alpha$: energy safety multiplier ($=\overline{\xi}$ if energy uncertainty enabled, else $1$).
    \item $e_{uv}$, $\tau_{uv}$: deterministic energy and time for $(u,v)$; $r_c,\eta_c$: charge rate and efficiency for charger $c$.
    \item $\mathcal{H}$: allowable charge durations (upper bound for continuous variables that are later rounded up).
    \item $M$: big-$M$ constant derived from worst-case energy and maximal charge addition.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Decision variables}
\begin{align*}
&b_k &&\text{battery before serving leg }k,\; k=0,\dots,K,\\
&x_k &&\in\{0,1\}\text{ direct-travel indicator for leg }k,\\
&y_{k,c} &&\in\{0,1\}\text{ use-charger indicator (at most one) for leg }k,\\
&t_{k,c} &&\ge 0\text{ continuous charge time at charger }c\text{ during leg }k,\\
&t_0 &&\ge 0\text{ optional initial charge time if }d_0\in\mathcal{C}.
\end{align*}

\subsection*{Objective}
\[
\min \; t_0 + \sum_{k=0}^{K-1}\Big( x_k \tau_{d_k,d_{k+1}} + \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}} y_{k,c}(\tau_{d_k,c}+\tau_{c,d_{k+1}}) + \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}} t_{k,c}\Big).
\]
This minimizes nominal travel plus charging time; solver tolerates a $10\%$ MIP gap and a $120$ s limit.

\subsection*{Constraints}
\begin{align*}
&b_0 =
    \begin{cases}
        b^{\text{init}} + r_c\eta_c t_0, & d_0=c\in\mathcal{C},\\
        b^{\text{init}}, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    &&\text{(initial battery)}\\
&x_k + \sum_{c\in\mathcal{C}} y_{k,c} = 1, &&\forall k \quad\text{(one routing choice per leg)}\\
&b_k - \alpha e_{d_k,d_{k+1}} \ge 0 - M(1-x_k), &&\forall k \quad\text{(energy for direct leg)}\\
&b_{k+1} = b_k - \alpha e_{d_k,d_{k+1}} + M(1-x_k), &&\forall k \quad\text{(battery update if direct)}\\
&b_k - \alpha e_{d_k,c} \ge 0 - M(1-y_{k,c}), &&\forall k,c \quad\text{(reach charger)}\\
&b_{k+1} = b_k - \alpha e_{d_k,c} - \alpha e_{c,d_{k+1}} + \eta_c r_c t_{k,c} + M(1-y_{k,c}), &&\forall k,c \quad\text{(battery via charger)}\\
&0 \le t_{k,c} \le \max \mathcal{H}\, y_{k,c}, &&\forall k,c \quad\text{(charge only if chosen)}\\
&\underline{b} \le b_k \le B, &&\forall k \quad\text{(bounds, with } \underline{b}=0.1B \text{ buffer)}.
\end{align*}
If no feasible routing exists, the policy falls back to an emergency plan (navigate to nearest reachable charger and charge to 95\%). Continuous $t_{k,c}$ and $t_0$ are rounded up to the nearest duration in $\mathcal{H}$ (with a $10\%$ buffer) when actions are emitted. Note that this formulation is a \emph{lower bound} on performance in the stochastic multi-truck environment, since it ignores charger contention and travel-time uncertainty.

%=============================================================================
\section{Solution Approach: PPO with Heterogeneous GNN}\label{sec:solution}
%=============================================================================

\subsection{GNN Architecture}

The policy and value networks share a \textbf{heterogeneous GNN feature encoder}:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Input projection}: Per-node-type linear layers (truck: $14\!\to\!d$, delivery: $3\!\to\!d$, charger: $4\!\to\!d$; default $d=64$).
    \item \textbf{Message passing}: $L$ \texttt{HeteroInteractionLayer}s (default $L=3$), each applying an \texttt{EdgeConditionedConv} per edge type. Messages are computed as:
    \[
    \mathbf{m}_{j\to i} = \text{MLP}\big([\mathbf{h}_i \| \mathbf{h}_j \| \mathbf{e}_{ij}]\big),
    \]
    aggregated via sum, and used to update node embeddings:
    \[
    \mathbf{h}_i' = \text{MLP}\big([\mathbf{h}_i \| \textstyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}(i)} \mathbf{m}_{j\to i}]\big).
    \]
    Multi-relational messages from different edge types targeting the same node type are averaged.
    \item \textbf{Global pooling}: Mean pool per node type $\to$ concatenated graph embedding $\in\mathbb{R}^{3d}$ (one $d$-vector per type).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Actor (Policy) Head}

Two variants are implemented:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Fixed action space}: Per-node-type output heads $\mathbf{h}_i \mapsto$ score; charging score from graph embedding.
    \item \textbf{Variable action space} (ActionGraphHead): Builds a fully-connected GCN over \emph{feasible actions only}, with action features $[\text{action\_type},\, \text{resulting\_SOC},\, \text{charge\_duration}]$. Two GCN layers produce per-action logits. This naturally accommodates varying numbers of feasible actions across steps and problem sizes.
\end{itemize}
Infeasible actions receive $-\infty$ logits (masked out before softmax).

\subsection{Critic (Value) Head}

MLP from the $3d$-dimensional graph embedding to a scalar state value $V(s)$. In the TD3-style variant, action/charging-duration indicators are concatenated to node features before encoding, and a twin-critic architecture is used.

\subsection{Training}

\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item PPO with GAE ($\gamma$, $\lambda$), entropy bonus (default $0.1$), clipped surrogate objective.
    \item 256 steps per update, 5 PPO epochs per update.
    \item Wandb integration for logging.
\end{itemize}

%=============================================================================
\section{Curriculum Learning}\label{sec:curriculum}
%=============================================================================

To train a single policy that generalizes across problem scales ($1$--$100$ trucks, $3$--$10$ stops), three curriculum strategies are supported:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Uniform}: Sample $(T, K) \sim \text{Uniform}([T_{\min}, T_{\max}] \times [K_{\min}, K_{\max}])$ per episode.
    \item \textbf{Staged}: Progress through predefined difficulty stages (e.g., $1\text{T}/3\text{S} \to 10\text{T}/3\text{S} \to 100\text{T}/3\text{S}$), advancing when a performance threshold is met.
    \item \textbf{Mixed}: Sample from multiple difficulty levels with specified frequency weights.
\end{itemize}

\section{Key Distinctions from Standard VRP}
%=============================================================================

\begin{center}
