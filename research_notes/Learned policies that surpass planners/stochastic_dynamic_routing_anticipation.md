# Anticipatory methods versus rolling-horizon re-optimisation in stochastic dynamic vehicle routing (emphasis: electric vehicles, dynamic pickup and delivery, drayage)

Evidence tags used below: **[FT]** = number read in the full text during this research; **[ABS]** = stated in the paper's abstract (publisher/OpenAlex/IDEAS record); **[SUM]** = taken from a search-engine summary of the abstract or a secondary source, full text not opened; **[TLDR]** = machine-generated one-line summary on Semantic Scholar, abstract not available. Anything recalled from memory and not re-checked is in the Gaps subsections only.

## 1. In which problem classes does anticipation add measurable value over myopic re-optimisation, and how large is it? Where is the value close to zero?

### Takeaway
Reported gains over a myopic or deterministic re-optimiser are large (roughly 10 to 30 %, occasionally more) when requests are highly dynamic and the fleet is resource-constrained so that accept/postpone/assign decisions shape future capacity, and small (0 to 5 %, sometimes negative) when the degree of dynamism is low, resources are ample, the uncertainty is in demands or travel/energy rather than in which jobs exist, or the baseline already samples the future. Several top-journal papers state explicitly that the value of anticipation shrinks with more vehicles, higher request rates or lower dynamism.

### Cited Findings

**Stochastic customer requests (dynamic VRP/VRPTW, same-day delivery)**
- Bent and Van Hentenryck (2004, Operations Research 52(6):977-987). Problem: dynamic VRPTW with stochastic customers, maximise customers served, Solomon-based instances with 30 to 80 % degree of dynamism. Method: multiple scenario approach (MSA) with a consensus function. Baseline: approaches not using stochastic information. Result: "dramatic improvements", consensus "improves the quality of the solutions significantly", and "the benefits of MSA increase with the (effective) degree of dynamism" [ABS]. No percentages in the abstract. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1040.0124)
- Joe and Lau (2020, ICAPS 30(1):394-402). Problem: dynamic VRPTW with known and stochastic customers as a route-based MDP; real logistics data, 48 customer locations, 2 to 4 vehicles, 30 to 40 daily orders. Method: DRLSA = neural TD value function + simulated annealing routing heuristic. Baseline: "pure re-optimization method i.e. myopic approach", approximate value iteration (AVI) and MSA. Result [FT]: improvement over myopic of −1.95 % at DoD 0.3, −0.98 % at DoD 0.5, +11.90 % at DoD 0.7 (Table 2); +1.92 % at DoD 0.6 and +12.72 % at DoD 0.8 (Table 4); 15.71 % and 20.88 % with 3 and 4 vehicles; versus MSA on DoD 0.7: DRLSA +9.61 % over myopic, MSA +1.73 % (50 samples) and +2.20 % (100 samples). The authors state DRLSA "does not perform better than myopic when DoD ≤ 0.5" because "anticipating a rarely occurring event becomes more challenging". — [Source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6685/6685-40-9914-1-10-20200521.pdf)
- Joe and Lau's reading of the Ritzinger, Puchinger and Hartl (2016, IJPR 54(1):215-231) survey: for instances with more than 30 customers "most approaches managed to achieve improvements in the region of 5%-10% over myopic" [FT of Joe and Lau, secondary for the survey]. The survey abstract itself says it analyses "the difference in solution quality" between purely dynamic/stochastic approaches and those using both [ABS]. — [Source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6685/6685-40-9914-1-10-20200521.pdf); [Survey](https://doi.org/10.1080/00207543.2015.1043403)
- Zhang, Luo, Florio and Van Woensel (2023, EJOR 306(2):596-614; arXiv 2202.12983). Problem: dynamic VRP with stochastic requests, maximise accepted requests, real street network with over 16,000 nodes, up to 20 vehicles and 900 expected dynamic requests. Method: knapsack-based linear approximations of the reward-to-go ("potential-based policies", PbP and S-PbP) plus anticipatory offline plans. Baseline: greedy acceptance with cheapest insertion (GP-CI), greedy with route re-optimisation (GP-R), a tuned policy function approximation (PFA), and rollouts of both. Results [FT, Tables 5 and 7]: (a) adding route re-optimisation to the greedy policy changes the acceptance rate by +2.8 % (small instances, mostly not significant) and −0.4 % (large instances, not significant); (b) PbP improves acceptance over GP-CI by +27.1 % (small) and +21.2 % (large); S-PbP +23.9 % and +15.6 %; PFA +17.7 to +19.4 % (small) and +8.9 % (large); (c) anticipatory offline plans add +3.6 % and +5.0 % on top of S-PbP and PbP, but 0.0 % on the smallest instance (2 vehicles, low request rate). — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12983)
- Baty, Jungel, Klein, Parmentier and Schiffer (2024, Transportation Science 58(4):708-725; arXiv 2304.00789). Problem: dynamic VRPTW with dispatching waves (EURO Meets NeurIPS 2022 competition). Method: neural network predicts request prizes, a prize-collecting VRPTW solver (HGS) is the combinatorial layer, trained by imitation of an anticipative (hindsight) policy. Baselines: greedy, lazy, random (competition baselines), single-scenario "rolling-horizon" lookahead, Monte Carlo multi-scenario consensus. Results [FT]: cost gap to the anticipative baseline is 74.05 % (lazy), 36.29 % (random), 20.99 % (greedy), 7.12 % (rolling-horizon with one sampled scenario), 6.97 % (Monte Carlo), 5.15 % (ML-CO); "Our ML-CO policy performs best across all online benchmark policies and outperforms the monte-carlo policy by 1.57%"; the authors note the improvement of Monte Carlo over one-scenario rolling horizon "is rather small in comparison to the improvement from the greedy policy to the rolling-horizon policy". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.00789)
- Lan, van Doorn, Wouda, Rijal and Bhulai (2024, Transportation Science 58(4):726-740; arXiv 2308.14476). Same competition problem. Method: iterative conditional dispatch (ICD), an iterative sample-scenario heuristic with a consensus function. Baselines: single-scenario rolling horizon (RH), the Hvattum et al. hedging heuristic (DSHH). Results [FT via ar5iv]: average gap to hindsight at 600 expected requests is RH 9.58 %, DSHH 8.76 %, ICD-double 8.05 %; at 300 requests RH 12.58 %, ICD-double 10.03 %; the gain from multiple scenarios is smallest on deadline instances and unimodal wide-window instances, where RH "stays competitive". — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.14476)
- Chen, Ulmer and Thomas (2022, EJOR 298(3):939-952; arXiv 1910.11901). Problem: same-day delivery with vehicles and drones, maximise customers served. Method: deep Q-learning over assign-to-drone / assign-to-vehicle / reject; routing by heuristics. Baseline: parametric policy function approximations (PFA) from the literature and improved variants. Results [FT]: DQL "improves upon πPFA by up to 22%"; "The value of πQ is greatest in the cases in which resources are more constrained" (more than 20 % with 2 vehicles and 5 drones, where PFA serves about 45 % of customers); "In only a very few cases, πPFA achieves better results than πQ ... Given sufficient resources, the PFA is an effective policy". — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11901)
- Voccia, Campbell and Thomas (2019, Transportation Science 53(1):167-184). Problem: multi-vehicle same-day delivery (dynamic pickup and delivery with time constraints). Method: sample-scenario planning with a consensus function and a waiting result. Result [ABS]: "the anticipation of future requests is most valuable when many requests' time windows occur late in the day" and "the value of anticipating the future decreases when the number of vehicles or the arrival rate of requests increases". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2016.0732)
- Ulmer (2019, Networks 73(3):277-291). Problem: single-vehicle dynamic VRP with stochastic requests under real-time limits. Method: compares an existing reactive re-optimisation method, an existing anticipation method (value function approximation), and a tunable combination. Result [ABS]: "the appropriate optimization combination is strongly connected to the degree of dynamism" and "our combination does not provide significant benefit compared to the respectively best optimization dimension". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/net.21861)
- Ghiani, Manni and Thomas (2012, Transportation Science 46(3):374-387). Problem: dynamic stochastic TSP. Result [ABS]: the cheap "anticipatory insertion" heuristic "can often match the quality of sample-scenario planning, particularly when the degree of dynamism is low". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1110.0374)
- Ghiani, Manni, Quaranta and Triki (2009, TR-E 45(1):96-106). Problem: same-day courier pickup and delivery. Method: short-term demand sampling with indifference-zone selection, including idle-vehicle relocation. Result [SUM]: anticipatory algorithms "provide consistently better solutions than their reactive counterparts"; no percentage in the abstract. — [Source](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:transe:v:45:y:2009:i:1:p:96-106)
- Schilde, Doerner and Hartl (2011, C&OR 38(12):1719-1730). Problem: dynamic stochastic dial-a-ride with expected return transports (Austrian Red Cross). Method: dynamic VNS, stochastic VNS, multiple plan and multiple scenario approaches. Result [ABS]: "using the stochastic information on return transports leads to average improvements of around 15%. Moreover, improvements of up to 41% can be achieved for some test instances." — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2011.02.006)
- Tirado, Hvattum, Fagerholt and Cordeau (2013, C&OR 40(1):253-263). Problem: dynamic stochastic industrial ship routing. Result [ABS]: stochastic information "yields average cost savings of 2.5%". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cor.2012.06.011)
- Hvattum, Løkketangen and Laporte (2007, Networks 49(4):330-340): branch-and-regret heuristic "shown to be superior to previous heuristics that are uniquely based on a pure dynamic approach" [ABS]; the 2006 hedging-heuristic paper (Transportation Science 40(4):421-438) gives no number in its abstract [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1002/net.20182); [2006 paper](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1060.0166)
- Jungel, Parmentier, Schiffer and Vidal (INFORMS Journal on Computing 38(3):745-765, online 2025). Problem: autonomous mobility-on-demand dispatch and rebalancing at city scale. Method: combinatorial-optimisation-enriched ML learned from full-information solutions. Baselines: greedy and model-predictive control. Result [ABS]: "up to 17.1% and on average by 6.3% in terms of realized profit, and on average by 4.7% in terms of satisfied customers". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2024.0637)
- Li et al. (2021, IEEE ICDE, pp. 2511-2522). Problem: industry-scale dynamic pickup and delivery (Huawei). Method: demand forecast + graph value function + double DQN. Baselines: deployed heuristic and vanilla DRL. Result [ABS]: "reduces 11.27% number of the used vehicles and decreases 13.12% total transportation cost on average". — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1109/icde51399.2021.00283)
- Klapp, Erera and Toriello (2018, Transportation Science 52(2):402-415), one-dimensional dynamic dispatch waves: "fully dynamic policies may perform arbitrarily better than a priori ones" [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2016.0682)

**Stochastic demand (uncertainty in quantities, not in which customers exist)**
- Secomandi (2001, Operations Research 49(5):796-802): rollout on an a-priori tour gives "1% to 4% average improvements on the a priori approach" [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.49.5.796.10608)
- Secomandi and Margot (2009, Operations Research 57(1):214-230): best partial re-optimisation heuristics are "on average no more than 10%-13% away" from a perfect-information lower bound on instances up to 100 customers [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1080.0520)
- Goodson, Ohlmann and Thomas (2013, Operations Research 61(1):138-154): rollout policies "improve upon the performance of a rolling horizon procedure and commonly employed fixed-route policies, with improvement over the latter being more substantial" [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/opre.1120.1127)

**Electric vehicles**
- Basso, Kulcsár, Sanchez-Diaz and Qu (2022, TR-E 157:102496). Problem: single electric truck, 10 or 20 customers, 2 charging stations, stochastic energy consumption and dynamic requests (Luxembourg traffic model). Method: "safe" tabular Q-learning with reduced state, two-layer chance-constrained policy, rollout with tabu search to guide training. Baseline: deterministic online re-optimisation with tabu search and a battery margin. Results [FT]: average energy −4.14 % / −4.76 % (10 customers, without / with charging) and −5.18 % / −5.06 % (20 customers), maximum −12.02 %; conclusion "could save on average 4.8% (up to 12%) energy ... compared with the deterministic online reoptimization approach"; under different dynamism settings the average saving ranges from −3.26 % to −5.64 % (Table 9); gap to the a-posteriori route is 10.24 % for re-optimisation and 7.09 % for RL (Table 8); the re-optimiser needed a 20 % battery margin to avoid depletion, RL had zero depletion episodes. — [Source](https://research.chalmers.se/publication/527406/file/527406_Fulltext.pdf)
- Kullman, Goodson and Mendoza (2021, Transportation Science 55(3):637-659): value of the routing policies is "within 10% of a dual bound" on real industry instances and they "significantly outperform the industry-standard routing strategy in which vehicle recharging generally occurs at a central depot" [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2020.1018)
- Dastpak, Errico, Jabali and Malucelli (2024, TR-C 158:104411; arXiv 2305.11773): re-optimising with real-time binary occupancy indicators reduces waiting times by 23.7 to 95.4 % and total trip duration by 1.4 to 18.5 % on average versus a benchmark that only observes station status on arrival [SUM of the arXiv abstract]. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.11773)

**Trucking, drayage, long haul**
- Powell, Towns and Marar (2000, Transportation Science 34(1)): "(myopic) optimal solutions only slightly outperform greedy solutions under relatively high levels of uncertainty, and ... a particular suboptimal solution actually outperforms optimal solutions under a wide range of conditions" [TLDR]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.34.1.67.12283)
- Tjokroamidjojo, Kutanoglu and Taylor (2006, TR-E): advance load information helps, but there is a "minimal cost difference between intelligent preplanning and the best performing dispatching policies" [TLDR]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2005.01.001)
- Máhr, Srour, De Weerdt and Zuidwijk (2010, TR-C 18(1):99-119), drayage with time windows: an agent-based auction system outperforms on-line MIP re-optimisation (every 30 s) under high service-time uncertainty and moderate combined uncertainty; on-line optimisation is competitive under job-arrival uncertainty and wins under extremely high combined uncertainty [repository abstract]. — [Source](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:fa55a499-d707-4e96-b1b9-dbeec53413ef)
- Zhang, Smilowitz and Erera (2011, TR-E 47(5):764-777), dynamic drayage with flexible tasks: "a drayage firm can find high-quality plans even when many tasks are not known initially" [SUM]. — [Source](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transe/v47y2011i5p764-777.html)
- Pérez Rivera and Mes (2019, Flexible Services and Manufacturing Journal 31(3):763-806): integrating drayage (MILP) with long-haul (MDP) scheduling gives "average cost savings between 4 and 24% on networks with a majority of pre-haulage freights" versus a non-integrated approach [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10696-019-09336-9)
- Alcaraz, Losilla and Caballero-Arnaldos (2022, TR-E 164:102790), long-distance routes with driver breaks and rests under uncertain travel and service times: online lookahead + rollout + MCTS "outperforms not only the base policy (up to 83%), but also a policy obtained offline using deep Q networks" [ABS]. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tre.2022.102790)

### Inferences
- The literature's regime map is consistent: value of anticipation rises with (i) degree of dynamism, (ii) scarcity of fleet capacity relative to demand, (iii) irreversibility of the decision (accept/reject, dispatch-now/postpone, which vehicle), and (iv) late or tight deadlines. It falls when most work is known up front, when capacity is ample, and when a plan can be cheaply revised later.
- Mapping to the five truck categories in the brief: deterministic-plan long haul resembles the low-dynamism regime (expected headroom about 0 to 5 %, in line with the 0.4 to 3 % planner gap); long haul with shared charging queues resembles Kullman et al. (2021) and Dastpak et al. (2024), where the gain comes from reacting to observed queue state rather than from learning; same-day pickup and delivery with a strong insertion planner resembles the Baty/Lan setting, where the learned method beats a sampling planner by under 2 %; drayage with dynamic requests and deadlines resembles the resource-constrained, high-dynamism regime where 10 to 30 % gains in services are routinely reported (Zhang et al. 2023, Chen et al. 2022, Joe and Lau 2020, Schilde et al. 2011). The 11 to 39 % drayage result in the brief is therefore within the range the literature reports for that regime, and the near-zero result elsewhere is also what the literature reports.
- Converting Baty et al.'s gaps: ML-CO cost is about 13 % below greedy and about 1.8 % below single-scenario rolling horizon (1.0515 versus 1.2099 and 1.0712 times the anticipative cost). Most of the value comes from any lookahead at all; the learned component adds a small final increment.
- Zhang et al. (2023) show that making the routing part of a myopic planner better (re-optimisation instead of cheapest insertion) is worth about 0 to 3 %, whereas anticipating capacity use is worth 15 to 27 %. In high-dynamism problems the headroom lies in the acceptance/assignment/timing decision, not in tighter routes.

- Exact percentages for Bent and Van Hentenryck (2004), Hvattum et al. (2006), Ichoua et al. (2006), Mitrović-Minić et al. (2004), Ghiani et al. (2009), Goodson et al. (2006) is usually quoted as reducing travel distance by about 15 % versus a myopic dynamic heuristic.
- Ritzinger et al. (2016) contains a graphical comparison of improvements across papers; only its abstract and Joe and Lau's paraphrase were read.
- Soeffker, Ulmer and Mattfeld (2022, EJOR 298(3):801-820, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejor.2021.07.014) derive "recommendations about promising methodology" per problem type [ABS]; the body with the actual recommendations could not be opened.

## 2. Which method designs win in the literature: learned policy alone, learned value function inside a search or routing heuristic, rollout on a heuristic base policy, or scenario sampling inside the planner?

### Takeaway
In the operations research and transportation journals the winning designs are hybrids: a learned or approximated value (or learned cost/prize parameters) steering a combinatorial planner, or a scenario/rollout lookahead wrapped around a routing heuristic. End-to-end learned policies that construct routes directly have no verified win over a strong re-optimiser for multi-vehicle problems with complex route constraints, and the main position paper says so explicitly.

### Cited Findings
- Modelling frame. Ulmer, Goodson, Mattfeld and Thomas (2020, EURO J. Transp. Logist. 9(2):100008): route-based MDPs "augment the state space, action space, and reward structure to include routing information", which makes it "conceptually easier to connect dynamic routing problems with the route-based methods typically used to solve them" [ABS]. Carrying the current route plan in the state is the standard formal device. — [Source](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejtl.2020.100008)
- Position paper. Hildebrandt, Thomas and Ulmer (2023, C&OR 150:106071; arXiv 2103.00507) [FT via ar5iv]: "Exact MIP solvers search the action space effectively; RL methods evaluate actions well with respect to the future"; existing work "restrict[s] the complex action space by solving only parts by RL and everything else by established heuristics"; for policy-based route construction "there is no example where such an approach proved effective for larger fleets"; "There is no work in OR that considers a detailed action space for SDVRPs with complex route constraints"; proposed directions are piecewise-linear neural value functions embedded in MIP, policy-based search with a feasibility head, and multi-agent evaluation with "a global MIP searching the joint action space"; training cost "is still a limiting factor" and policies should be trained "on small instances before transferring them". — [Source](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2103.00507)
- Learned value function + routing heuristic. Joe and Lau (2020): TD-learned value of the remaining route cost + simulated annealing beats MSA (9.61 % versus 1.73 to 2.20 % over myopic) at under 1 minute versus 3 to 10 minutes per scenario [FT]. Requests are pre-assigned to vehicles in their setting (noted by Hildebrandt et al.). — [Source](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/6685/6685-40-9914-1-10-20200521.pdf)
- Learned value function for the assignment decision, heuristics for routing. Chen, Ulmer and Thomas (2022): up to 22 % over PFA [FT]. Ulmer, Thomas and Mattfeld (2019, EJTL 8(4):327-361) "couple an approximation of the value of choosing any particular subset of requests for delivery with a routing heuristic" [ABS]. Ulmer, Mattfeld and Köster (2018, Transportation Science 52(1):20-37): value function over (time, free time budget) outperforms "state-of-the-art benchmark heuristics" [ABS]. — [Chen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.11901); [Ulmer 2019](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13676-018-0124-0); [Ulmer 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2016.0719)
- Structured value approximation + exact assignment rules. Zhang et al. (2023): knapsack-based reward-to-go approximations beat both PFA and sampled rollouts in acceptance rate and decision time [FT]. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12983)
- Learned parameters + combinatorial optimisation layer (imitation of hindsight solutions). Baty et al. (2024) ranked first in the EURO Meets NeurIPS 2022 dynamic track [ABS] and argue that RL alone "struggle[s] to perform well on high-dimensional combinatorial problems" [ABS]; Jungel et al. (IJOC) report up to 17.1 % profit gain over greedy and MPC [ABS]. — [Baty](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2023.0107); [Jungel](https://doi.org/10.1287/ijoc.2024.0637)
- Scenario sampling inside the planner. Bent and Van Hentenryck (2004) [ABS]; Hvattum et al. (2006, 2007) [ABS]; Schilde et al. (2011), 15 % average [ABS]; Srour, Agatz and Oppen (2018, Transportation Science 52(1):3-19): for pickup and delivery with time windows and temporal uncertainty, "it is beneficial to tailor the consensus function ... to the specifics of the problem setting" [ABS]; Lan et al. (2024): a pure sample-scenario method placed second in the competition, 0.8 % above the best entry at 120 s per epoch, 0.3 % at 600 s, "nearly matching the performance of the winning machine learning-based strategy" [FT/ABS]. — [Srour](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2015.0658); [Lan](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2023.0111)
- Anticipation by plan shaping without sampling (waiting, relocation, dummy customers, double horizon). Ichoua, Gendreau and Potvin (2006, Transportation Science 40(2):211-225) insert "dummy customers (representing forecasted requests)" [ABS]; Branke et al. (2005, Transportation Science 39(3):298-312): "a proper waiting strategy can significantly increase the probability of being able to service the additional customer" [ABS]; Mitrović-Minić, Krishnamurti and Laporte (2004, TR-B 38(8):669-685): "computational results show the advantage of using a double-horizon in conjunction with insertion and improvement heuristics" [SUM]; Thomas (2007, Transportation Science 41(3):319-331): waiting heuristic from MDP structure [ABS]. — [Ichoua](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1050.0114); [Branke](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1040.0095); [Mitrović-Minić](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/transb/v38y2004i8p669-685.html); [Thomas](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1060.0183)
- Tuned re-optimisation (cost function approximation, CFA). Perkins and Powell (arXiv 1703.04644) argue that deterministic rolling-horizon models with buffers and slack are "parametrically modified cost function approximations" whose parameters can be tuned in a stochastic simulator, a legitimate policy class rather than a flawed approximation [abstract]. Horváth, Kis and Györgyi (2025, Multimodal Transportation 4(1):100194): a dynamic pickup-and-delivery planner with a perturbed objective (penalties on waiting times and idle vehicles) solved by VNS "significantly surpasses the current state-of-the-art methods" on "a widely adopted benchmark dataset" [ABS]. Ulmer, Thomas, Campbell and Woyak (2021, Transportation Science 55(1):75-100): the anticipatory customer assignment policy postpones selected assignments and adds a time buffer, improving service "significantly for all stakeholders compared with current practice" [ABS]. — [Perkins and Powell](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.04644); [Horváth](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.multra.2025.100194); [Ulmer 2021](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2020.1000)
- Rollout on a heuristic base policy. See question 3; verified wins in VRP with stochastic demand (Secomandi 2001; Goodson et al. 2013, 2016), EV routing with station queues (Kullman et al. 2021), orienteering on queues (Zhang, Ohlmann and Thomas 2018), charging-station search (Guillet et al. 2022), driver break/rest scheduling under uncertainty (Alcaraz et al. 2022).
- Learned policy alone. Bono et al. (2021, IEEE T-ITS 22(12):7804-7813): attention policy shows "promising results in these dynamic and stochastic environments, while staying competitive in deterministic ones compared to offline classical heuristics" on artificial data [ABS]. Kullman, Cousineau, Goodson and Mendoza (2022, Transportation Science 56(3):775-794): deep Q-learning policies for electric ride-hailing; "across instances of varying size, our best policy trained with deep reinforcement learning outperforms the reoptimization approach" and "may be effectively scaled and deployed on larger instances without retraining" [ABS]. Ardali and Knapp (arXiv 2608.13799, 2026 preprint, not peer reviewed): masked MLP/Transformer policies trained by behaviour cloning then PPO for dynamic multi-depot routing "did not outperform the strongest heuristic"; PPO "had little average effect on the MLP"; rolling horizon gave the lowest waiting and makespan at much higher compute [arXiv abstract summary]. — [Bono](https://doi.org/10.1109/TITS.2020.3009289); [Kullman 2022](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.2021.1042); [Ardali and Knapp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13799)
- Large-scale truckload. Simão et al. (2009, Transportation Science 43(2):178-197): approximate dynamic programming with value functions inside assignment LPs for over 6,000 drivers; "The goal of the model was not to obtain a better solution but rather to closely match a number of operational statistics" [ABS]. Spivey and Powell (2004, Transportation Science 38(4)): an "adaptive, nonmyopic algorithm that involves iteratively solving sequences of assignment problems no larger than what would be required of a myopic model" [TLDR]. — [Simão](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1080.0238); [Spivey and Powell](https://doi.org/10.1287/trsc.1030.0073)

### Inferences
- Ranking by weight of verified evidence: (1) value or cost/prize approximation steering a combinatorial planner; (2) scenario sampling with consensus inside the planner; (3) rollout of a heuristic base policy where one episode of simulation is cheap; (4) tuned re-optimisation (CFA), under-represented in learning papers but competitive where tried; (5) end-to-end learned route construction, with no verified win over a strong re-optimiser in a multi-vehicle constrained setting.
- A policy that receives the planner's plan as input and chooses among a variable-size action set is, in Hildebrandt et al.'s terms, RL evaluating actions that an OR method has searched. The literature would call the strongest version of it a learned value (or advantage) over planner-proposed actions, not a stand-alone policy.
- Imitation of hindsight-optimal decisions (Baty; Jungel) is a different teacher from imitation of the online planner. Imitating the online planner caps performance at the planner; imitating full-information solutions gives a target that is above the planner by construction. This matches the "slightly worse copy" observation in the brief.
- Kullman et al. (2022) is the main top-journal example where a mostly learned policy beats re-optimisation, and its action is a per-vehicle assignment/recharge/reposition choice, not route construction.

## 3. What do rollout and offline-online ADP guarantee relative to the base policy, and what compute do they need per decision?

### Takeaway
With exact evaluation, rollout is guaranteed to be no worse than a base policy that is sequentially consistent or sequentially improving, and offline-online ADP inherits this when the value-function base policy consists of deterministic decision rules. With sampled evaluation the guarantee is only statistical, and published multi-vehicle experiments show sampled rollouts costing 10 to 150+ seconds per decision and sometimes failing to beat their base policy.

### Cited Findings
- Bertsekas, Tsitsiklis and Wu (1997, Journal of Heuristics 3(3):245-262) "gave conditions under which the rollout algorithm is guaranteed to perform as well as its base policy" (sequential consistency, sequential improvement), as summarised in a later rollout analysis (arXiv 1301.4529) [SUM; the original paper was not opened]. — [Source](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1301.4529v2); [Original](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1009635226865)
- Bertsekas, constrained rollout: "if the base heuristic produces a feasible solution, the rollout algorithm has a cost improvement property", i.e. it returns a feasible solution with cost no worse than the base heuristic [SUM of the paper's abstract]. — [Source](https://www.mit.edu/~dimitrib/Rollout_Constrained.pdf)
- Goodson, Thomas and Ohlmann (2017, EJOR 258(1):216-229): "We formalize rollout variants exploiting the pre- and post-decision state variables" to handle large state and action spaces; a unified analysis generalises earlier results and relates the variants' performance; illustrated on a dynamic stochastic multi-compartment knapsack problem [IDEAS abstract]. — [Source](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/ejores/v258y2017i1p216-229.html)
- Goodson, Ohlmann and Thomas (2013): fixed-route (a-priori) base policies, one-step plus pre- and post-decision rollout, and "a dynamic decomposition scheme that achieves high quality solutions to large problem instances with reasonable computational effort" [ABS]. - Alcaraz et al. ### Inferences
- The improvement guarantee is a statement about exact expected values. It transfers to practice only when (a) the base policy is a deterministic function of the state (a time-limited randomised metaheuristic planner is not, unless seeded and budgeted deterministically), and (b) enough sample paths are used that the comparison between candidate actions is statistically reliable. Zhang et al. (2023) is direct evidence that few-sample rollouts lose the guarantee in practice.
- Cost arithmetic for the simulator in the brief (0.05 to 2 s per decision, 100 to 1,400 decisions per episode): one full-episode base-policy simulation costs between about 5 s and 2,800 s. A one-step rollout over |A| candidate actions with H sample paths costs |A|·H such simulations per decision. Full-horizon rollout is therefore out of reach except on the smallest configurations; truncated rollouts with a learned terminal value (Ulmer 2020), post-decision/hybrid variants that reduce the number of evaluated actions (Goodson et al. 2017), or evaluating only two candidates (planner's action versus the learned policy's action) are the designs the literature points to.
- A "safeguarded" design follows directly from the rollout literature: deviate from the planner's action only when a (learned or simulated) advantage estimate is positive with margin. By the rollout argument this is no worse than the planner in expectation up to estimation error, which addresses the "at least match the planner in every category" requirement.
- Offline-online ADP was demonstrated on a single vehicle. No multi-vehicle offline-online result with a proven guarantee was found.

## 4. What has been shown for electric fleets with uncertain charging-station availability or queues, and for hours-of-service constraints under uncertainty?

### Takeaway
For uncertain charging availability the established results come from single-vehicle or decomposed models: dynamic policies that react to observed queues (rollout on fixed routes, re-optimisation with occupancy data) clearly beat static plans and are certified within about 10 % of dual bounds, while learned policies have shown about 5 % energy savings over deterministic re-optimisation plus better reliability. For hours-of-service rules under uncertainty the literature is thin: one TR-E paper uses online rollout/MCTS on a rule-based base policy, and the electric-truck work with rest rules is deterministic.

## 6. How do papers in the top transportation journals frame the contribution when the learned method does not dominate re-optimisation in every setting?

### Takeaway
They frame it as a conditional result with a mechanism: they map the regimes (degree of dynamism, resource tightness, uncertainty type) in which anticipation pays, certify closeness to optimal with dual or hindsight bounds where it does not, and claim secondary benefits (decision time, reliability, transfer across sizes, simplicity). Papers that report "no significant benefit" or "matches the simpler method" in some regimes are published in Transportation Science, Networks and EJOR.

### Inferences
- A defensible framing for a paper with one clear win (drayage) and ties elsewhere: (i) one event-driven MDP and one policy architecture across five problem categories; (ii) a regime map showing where headroom over the planner exists, backed by hindsight or best-known-solution gaps showing the planner is already within 0.4 to 3 % where the learned policy only ties; (iii) a guarantee-by-construction hybrid (planner-in-the-loop, deviate only on positive estimated advantage) so that "never worse than the planner" is a property, not an empirical hope; (iv) the drayage gain reported with its trade-off (more operating hours, more late jobs) on a multi-KPI table; (v) decision time per event against the planner's.
- The papers that were accepted with partial dominance all quantify the upper bound on what was achievable (dual, hindsight or perfect-information bounds). Reporting the best-known-solution gap per category serves that role here.
- Reviewers at these venues are likely to ask for (a) a sample-scenario or tuned-objective (CFA) version of the planner as a baseline, (b) results by degree of dynamism and fleet tightness, and (c) an ablation showing what the RL stage adds over imitation, since Baty et al. obtain their result with imitation alone and Ardali and Knapp report little average effect from PPO.
